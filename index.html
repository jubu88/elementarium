Elementarium

A falling-sand toy. Every pixel is a particle with a few simple rules — all the interesting stuff happens where they meet. There is no goal. Poke the world and see what it does.

Controls

  • draw — left mouse / touch
  • erase — right mouse
  • brush size — scroll, or [ ]
  • pause — space
  • clear — C
  • demo scene — D
  • elements — 1–0, eraser E
  • save world PNG — S
  • load world — L, or drop a PNG on the canvas
  • this panel — H

Things to try

  • Pour water onto lava — it quenches into stone under a burst of steam. You can build with it.
  • Drip lava onto sand to forge glass.
  • Drop seeds by a pond. Plants drink the water and grow.
  • Oil floats on water — and burns. Set the sea on fire.
  • Lay a thin trail of gunpowder to a stockpile, then light the far end.
  • Build a weather jar: lava pit and pond in a glass box, separated by a stone divider. The stone conducts heat and glows; the pond simmers; steam condenses on the lid and rains. Roof the lava with glass or the rain will slowly quench it to stone.
  • Fungus creeps over plants and wood. Fire stops it — at a price.
  • Acid eats almost everything, but not glass. Make a beaker.
  • Ice slowly freezes water it touches. Snow piles up, then melts.
  • Critters graze on plants, flee smoke, breed when well fed, and starve back into seeds. Build them a garden by a pond — then introduce a problem.
  • Draw a predator near a herd. It scents prey, gives chase, and hunts them down; the critters scatter in fear. Predators breed slowly, starve fast, and — like everything here — return to the soil as a seed.
  • Worlds save as PNG images — the picture is the save file. Share them, or paint new ones in any image editor with the palette colors.
